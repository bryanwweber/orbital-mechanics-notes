Elliptical Orbits (0 < e < 1)

If the eccentricity is between 0 and 1, then the radius of the orbit varies with the true anomaly. However, the magnitude of the product ecos⁡νe \cos\nuecosν is never greater than one. This means that the bottom of the fraction in the orbit equation, Eq. 123, is never zero and the orbit is an elliptical shape. The minimum value of rrr occurs at periapsis where ν=0\nu = 0ν=0 and the maximum value of rrr is at apoapsis where ν=π\nu = \piν=π. The distance to periapsis and apoapsis are given by Eq. 126 and Eq. 127, respectively.

Specific Energy¶

The specific energy of the orbit is found in terms of the semi-major axis as:

E=−μ2aE = -\frac{\mu}{2a}E=−2aμ​
(144)

which is the same formula as the circular orbit, with the radius equal to the semi-major axis. In addition, the specific energy depends only on the semi-major axis, and is independent of the eccentricity.

Period¶

Interestingly, there is no formula to calculate the perimeter of an ellipse. This was discussed recently by Matt Parker on Stand Up Maths, where I first found out about it.

The period of the elliptical orbit can be found in terms of the semi-major and semi-minor axes. The area of an ellipse is given by:

A=πabA = \pi a bA=πab
(145)
