Orbital Plane and Kepler’s Second Law

The two vectors r\vector{r}r and r˙\dot{\vector{r}}r˙ define a plane called the orbital plane. The angular momentum vector is perpendicular to this plane. The unit vector normal to the plane is:

h^=hh\uvec{h} = \frac{\vector{h}}{h}h^=hh​
(108)

where hhh is the magnitude of the specific angular momentum.

Since the angular momentum is constant for all time, it cannot change direction and it cannot change magnitude. Therefore, the unit vector perpendicular to the plane is constant for all time. This means that m2m_2m2​ always orbits in the same plane around m1m_1m1​.

Kepler’s Second Law¶

Now consider m2m_2m2​ in orbit around m1m_1m1​. As m2m_2m2​ moves, the position vector r\vector{r}r sweeps out an area inside the orbit. Over one whole orbit, the entire area inside the orbit will be swept. During a differential time interval dtdtdt, a differential area dAdAdA is swept. The rate of change of this area is:

dAdt=h2\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{h}{2}dtdA​=2h​
(112)

This is called the areal velocity. Since hhh is constant, because of conservation of angular momentum, dA/dtdA/dtdA/dt is also constant. This is Kepler’s Second Law:
