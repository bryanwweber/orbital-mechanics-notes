Relative Motion

Now, let’s apply some of these vector properties to something more related to the real world. Let PPP be a particle in arbitrary motion. We define two reference frames, one inertial and one moving.

The position of PPP relative to the inertial frame is given by r\vector{r}r and relative to the moving frame is rrel\vector{r}_{\text{rel}}rrel​. The position of the origin OOO of the moving reference frame relative to the inertial frame is rO\vector{r}_OrO​. It then follows that the relationship between these vectors is:

r=rO+rrel\vector{r} = \vector{r}_O + \vector{r}_{\text{rel}}r=rO​+rrel​
(466)

Taking the time derivative of r\vector{r}r gives the absolute velocity:

v=vO+Ω×rrel+vrel\vector{v} = \vector{v}_O + \vector{\Omega}\cross\vector{r}_{\text{rel}} + \vector{v}_{\text{rel}}v=vO​+Ω×rrel​+vrel​
(467)

and taking the time derivative of v\vector{v}v gives the absolute acceleration:

a=aO+Ω˙×rrel+Ω×(Ω×rrel)+2Ω×vrel\vector{a} = \vector{a}_O + \dot{\vector{\Omega}}\cross\vector{r}_{\text{rel}} + \vector{\Omega}\cross\left(\vector{\Omega}\cross\vector{r}_{\text{rel}}\right) + 2\vector{\Omega}\cross\vector{v}_{\text{rel}}a=aO​+Ω˙×rrel​+Ω×(Ω×rrel​)+2Ω×vrel​
(468)

The last cross-product term is known as the Coriolis acceleration.

Motion Relative to Earth Frames¶

The relative position vector of PPP to the center of the Earth is:

rrel=(RE+z)k^\vector{r}_{\text{rel}} = \left(R_E + z\right)\uvec{k}rrel​=(RE​+z)k^
(469)

Nonrotating Earth is a little weird... It means to assume that, for the purposes of these calculations, the Earth is not rotating. We’ll correct for the rotation in a minute.

where RER_ERE​ is the radius of the Earth (assumed constant for now) and zzz is the height of PPP above the surface of the Earth. The velocity of PPP relative to the nonrotating Earth is:

vrel=z˙k^+(RE+z)dk^dt\vector{v}_{\text{rel}} = \dot{z}\uvec{k} + \left(R_E + z\right)\frac{d\uvec{k}}{dt}vrel​=z˙k^+(RE​+z)dtdk^​
(470)

Absolute Motion¶

The absolute velocity, in the ECI frame, is:

v=vC+Ω×rrel+vrel\vector{v} = \vector{v}_C + \vector{\Omega}\cross\vector{r}_{\text{rel}} + \vector{v}_{\text{rel}}v=vC​+Ω×rrel​+vrel​
(477)

Geometrically, we find that K^=cos⁡ϕȷ^+sin⁡ϕk^\uvec{K} = \cos\phi\uvec{\jmath} + \sin\phi\uvec{k}K^=cosϕ^​+sinϕk^, which we can use to determine the angular velocity of the Earth:

Ω=ΩK^=Ωcos⁡ϕȷ^+Ωsin⁡ϕk^\vector{\Omega} = \Omega\uvec{K} = \Omega\cos\phi\uvec{\jmath} + \Omega\sin\phi\uvec{k}Ω=ΩK^=Ωcosϕ^​+Ωsinϕk^
(478)

In the ECI, vC=0\vector{v}_C = 0vC​=0, so we can find the absolute velocity of PPP:

v=[x˙+Ω(RE+z)cos⁡ϕ]ı^+y˙ȷ^+z˙k^\vector{v} = \left[\dot{x} + \Omega\left(R_E + z\right)\cos\phi\right]\uvec{\imath} + \dot{y}\uvec{\jmath} + \dot{z}\uvec{k}v=[x˙+Ω(RE​+z)cosϕ]^+y˙​^​+z˙k^
(479)

Next, the absolute acceleration of PPP is given by:

a=aC+Ω˙×rrel+Ω×(Ω×rrel)+2Ω×vrel+arel\vector{a} = \vector{a}_C + \dot{\vector{\Omega}}\cross\vector{r}_{\text{rel}} + \vector{\Omega}\cross\left(\vector{\Omega}\cross\vector{r}_{\text{rel}}\right) + 2\vector{\Omega}\cross\vector{v}_{\text{rel}} + \vector{a}_{\text{rel}}a=aC​+Ω˙×rrel​+Ω×(Ω×rrel​)+2Ω×vrel​+arel​
